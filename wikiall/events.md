## I. PRE-HISTORY (1649–1831)

### 1. Execution of Charles I (30 Jan 1649) — Revolution, Critical
**Location:** Whitehall, London | **People:** Oliver Cromwell, Charles I, Gerrard Winstanley
The English Revolution beheaded the divine right of kings and established parliamentary supremacy. Cromwell's New Model Army defeated Royalist forces and brought Charles I to public trial — the first time a reigning monarch was tried and executed by his own subjects. Simultaneously, the Diggers under Gerrard Winstanley occupied St. George's Hill demanding common ownership of land ("The Earth is a Common Treasury"), prefiguring communist ideas by two centuries.

### 2. Storming of the Bastille (14 Jul 1789) — Revolution, Critical
**Location:** Paris, France | **People:** Louis XVI
The *sans-culottes* stormed the Bastille, symbol of royal absolutism, marking the symbolic beginning of the French Revolution. The intervention of the Parisian crowd made the revolution irreversible. The revolution abolished feudal privileges, declared the Rights of Man, and ultimately executed the king — sending shockwaves across every European monarchy.

### 3. Haitian Revolution Begins (Aug 1791) — Revolution, Critical
**Location:** Saint-Domingue (Haiti) | **People:** Toussaint Louverture
The only successful large-scale slave revolt in history. Toussaint Louverture emerged as military and political leader, defeating French, Spanish, and British forces. The revolution forced the Enlightenment to confront its racial hypocrisy: "Rights of Man" had never been intended for the Black slave. It permanently altered the politics of slavery, colonialism, and race in the Atlantic world.

### 4. Execution of Louis XVI (21 Jan 1793) — Revolution, Major
**Location:** Paris, France | **People:** Louis XVI, Maximilien Robespierre
Marked the radicalization of the French Revolution. The Committee of Public Safety centralized power to defend the revolution against feudal Europe. The Terror (1793–94) remains debated: necessary defense or prototype of revolutionary authoritarianism. The execution sent a clear message to every monarchy in Europe.

### 5. Conspiracy of Equals (1796) — Revolution, Major
**Location:** Paris, France | **People:** Gracchus Babeuf, Sylvain Maréchal
First explicitly communist conspiracy in modern history. Babeuf demanded abolition of private property and a "community of goods." Betrayed and executed in 1797, but his ideas survived through Buonarroti's 1828 book. Marx and Engels acknowledged Babeuf as a forerunner — the bourgeois revolution, once begun, could generate demands far beyond the bourgeoisie's own programme.

### 6. Independence of Haiti (1 Jan 1804) — Revolution, Critical
**Location:** Haiti | **People:** Jean-Jacques Dessalines, Toussaint Louverture
Dessalines ripped the white stripe from the French tricolor. Haiti became the first independent Black republic. France demanded massive "reparations" for lost slave property — a debt Haiti paid until 1947, crippling its development. Haiti proved enslaved peoples could liberate themselves through organized action.

### 7. Defeat of Napoleon at Waterloo (18 Jun 1815) — Historical, Major
**Location:** Waterloo, Belgium | **People:** Napoleon Bonaparte
Ended the Napoleonic Wars and ushered in the Congress of Vienna's attempted Restoration. But the Napoleonic Code remained, embedding bourgeois property relations across Europe. Politically reactionary monarchies now presided over societies whose legal foundations had been permanently transformed — a contradiction generating the revolutions of 1830 and 1848.

### 8. Birth of Karl Marx (5 May 1818) — Biography, Critical
**Location:** Trier, Prussia | **People:** Karl Marx
Born into a middle-class family in the Prussian Rhineland, shaped by the French Revolution and Napoleonic occupation. Studied law and philosophy at Bonn and Berlin, encountered Hegelian philosophy, and developed the framework of historical materialism and the critique of political economy that transformed the workers' movement worldwide.

### 9. Birth of Friedrich Engels (28 Nov 1820) — Biography, Critical
**Location:** Barmen, Prussia | **People:** Friedrich Engels
Born into a wealthy textile manufacturing family. His father owned cotton mills in Germany and Manchester, giving Engels a direct view of industrial capitalism from both the factory floor and the boardroom. His *Condition of the Working Class in England* (1845) was one of the first systematic analyses of industrial poverty. Became Marx's closest collaborator and financial supporter.

### 10. First Capitalist Crisis (1825) — Economic, Normal
**Location:** England
First truly international capitalist economic crisis. Began with a speculative bubble collapse in Latin American stocks, spread across Europe and Latin America. Demonstrated that capitalism's instability is systemic, not accidental — confirming what became central to Marxist economics: periodic crises of overproduction driven by the contradiction between socialized production and private appropriation.

### 11. Silk Weavers' Revolt / Canuts (Nov 1831) — Revolution, Major
**Location:** Lyon, France
First independent working-class uprising in modern history. Lyon silk weavers seized the city under "Live working or die fighting." The black flag was flown for the first time as a symbol of proletarian hunger. Suppressed by the French army, but demonstrated the working class was capable of independent political and military action, distinct from the bourgeoisie.

## II. BIRTH OF THE THEORY (1843–1871)

### 12. Marx Marries Jenny von Westphalen (19 Jun 1843) — Biography, Normal
**Location:** Kreuznach, Prussia | **People:** Karl Marx, Jenny von Westphalen
Seven-year engagement. Marriage marked by deep devotion alongside extreme material hardship. Jenny shared Marx's political convictions, copied manuscripts, and maintained political correspondence. Her sacrifice was essential to Marx's ability to continue his theoretical work.

### 13. Marx and Engels Meet in Paris (Aug 1844) — Biography, Critical
**Location:** Paris, France | **People:** Karl Marx, Friedrich Engels
Met at the Café de la Régence. Discovered they had arrived at identical conclusions independently — Marx through philosophy and political economy, Engels through observation of English industrial conditions. Initiated the most important intellectual partnership in the history of the workers' movement.

### 14. Marx Writes The German Ideology (1845) — Publication, Major
**Location:** Brussels, Belgium | **People:** Karl Marx, Friedrich Engels
First systematic formulation of historical materialism. "The ruling ideas of each age have ever been the ideas of its ruling class." Unpublished in their lifetime — Marx said it served "self-clarification." Full text published 1932, transforming understanding of Marx's thought.

### 15. Communist League Founded (Jun 1847) — Organization, Major
**Location:** London, England | **People:** Karl Marx, Friedrich Engels
Emerged from the League of the Just. Adopted "Working Men of All Countries, Unite!" Marx and Engels were commissioned to write the programme — *The Communist Manifesto*. First international organization explicitly committed to the communist programme.

### 16. Communist Manifesto Published (21 Feb 1848) — Publication, Critical
**Location:** London, England | **People:** Karl Marx, Friedrich Engels
Most influential political pamphlet in history. "A spectre is haunting Europe — the spectre of communism." Analyzes the rise of the bourgeoisie, the nature of class struggle, and the historical mission of the proletariat. "The proletarians have nothing to lose but their chains. They have a world to win."

### 17. The Springtime of Nations (Mar 1848) — Revolution, Critical
**Location:** Europe | **People:** Karl Marx, Friedrich Engels
Revolutions erupted across Europe. Marx and Engels edited the *Neue Rheinische Zeitung* in Cologne as "the extreme left wing of democracy." Revealed that the bourgeoisie, confronted with proletarian demands, would ally with the old order. Provided material for Marx's most penetrating historical analyses.

### 18. June Days Uprising (Jun 1848) — Revolution, Critical
**Location:** Paris, France
Definitive class rupture of 1848. Workers rose when the government dissolved the National Workshops. ~3,000 killed, 1,500 summarily executed, 12,000 arrested. Marx called it "the first great battle between the two great classes of modern society." Bourgeoisie and proletariat parted ways definitively.

### 19. The Eighteenth Brumaire (1852) — Publication, Major
**Location:** London, England | **People:** Karl Marx
Marx's masterpiece of historical analysis. "History repeats itself, first as tragedy, then as farce." Introduces *Bonapartism* — where the executive achieves apparent independence from all classes. Develops the concept of the "dictatorship of the proletariat" as the necessary political form of transition.

### 20. Global Economic Crisis of 1857 — Economic, Normal
**Location:** Global | **People:** Karl Marx
First truly worldwide crisis. Marx saw it as a potential revolutionary trigger and intensified his economic studies, producing the *Grundrisse* notebooks (1857–58) — crucial preparatory stage for *Capital*.

### 21. First International Founded (28 Sep 1864) — Organization, Critical
**Location:** London, England | **People:** Karl Marx
International Workingmen's Association (IWA) founded at St. Martin's Hall. Marx delivered the Inaugural Address: "The emancipation of the working classes must be conquered by the working classes themselves." First sustained attempt to organize the workers' movement internationally.

### 22. Capital Volume I Published (14 Sep 1867) — Publication, Critical
**Location:** Hamburg, Germany | **People:** Karl Marx
"The most terrible missile that has yet been hurled at the heads of the bourgeoisie." Central discovery: surplus value — profit derives from exploitation of labor. Exploitation is not an abuse of the system but its essential mechanism. Foundational text of Marxist political economy.

### 23. SDAP Founded in Eisenach (1869) — Organization, Major
**Location:** Eisenach, Germany | **People:** August Bebel, Wilhelm Liebknecht
First mass political party based on Marxist principles. Distinguished from Lassalle's ADAV by internationalism and rejection of alliance with Bismarck. Affiliated with the First International. Model for socialist parties across Europe.

### 24. Birth of Vladimir Lenin (22 Apr 1870) — Biography, Critical
**Location:** Simbirsk, Russia | **People:** Vladimir Lenin
Born into a middle-class family. Brother Alexander executed in 1887 for plotting against the Tsar — the formative event of Lenin's youth. Would develop the theory of the vanguard party, lead the Bolshevik faction, and direct the October Revolution.

### 25. The Paris Commune Begins (18 Mar 1871) — Revolution, Critical
**Location:** Paris, France | **People:** Karl Marx
First workers' government in history. Abolished the standing army, made all officials elected and recallable, paid them workers' wages. Marx: "The working class cannot simply lay hold of the ready-made state machinery and wield it for its own purposes." Lasted 72 days.

### 26. Fall of the Paris Commune (28 May 1871) — Revolution, Critical
**Location:** Paris, France
Crushed during *Semaine Sanglante*. ~20,000 Communards killed or executed. Thousands deported to New Caledonia. The Commune's martyrs became sacred figures. Every subsequent revolutionary movement drew lessons from both its achievements and its defeat.

## III. FIRST INTERNATIONAL DISSOLUTION (1872–1876)

### 27. Hague Congress of the First International (Sep 1872) — Organization, Major
**Location:** The Hague, Netherlands | **People:** Karl Marx, Mikhail Bakunin, Friedrich Engels
Most contentious congress in IWA history. General Council relocated to New York; anarchist faction under Bakunin expelled. The Marx-Bakunin split centered on political action vs. immediate abolition of the state, and centralized party vs. federalist communes. Division persists to this day.

### 28. First International Dissolved (15 Jul 1876) — Organization, Major
**Location:** Philadelphia, USA | **People:** Karl Marx, Friedrich Engels
IWA formally dissolved. Despite organizational demise, it established the principle of international working-class solidarity and demonstrated that the class struggle is inherently international.

## IV. AGE OF IMPERIALISM & SECOND INTERNATIONAL (1875–1914)

### 29. Gotha Congress (1875) — Organization, Major
**Location:** Gotha, Germany | **People:** Karl Marx, Ferdinand Lassalle
United German socialism (SDAP + ADAV) into SAPD (later SPD). Marx's *Critique of the Gotha Programme* attacks reformist slogans and argues for the "revolutionary dictatorship of the proletariat." Published 1891 by Engels against SPD leadership wishes.

### 30. First Russian Translation of Manifesto (1882) — Publication, Normal
**Location:** Geneva, Switzerland | **People:** Georgi Plekhanov, Karl Marx
Marx's preface addresses whether Russia can bypass capitalism via the peasant commune (*obshchina*) — conditionally yes, if supported by Western revolution. Most explicit engagement with uneven development.

### 31. Death of Karl Marx (14 Mar 1883) — Biography, Critical
**Location:** London, England | **People:** Karl Marx, Friedrich Engels
Died stateless at 64. Buried at Highgate Cemetery. Engels: "His name will endure through the ages." Left vast unfinished manuscripts including materials for Capital Vols. II & III.

### 32. Das Kapital Volume II Published (1885) — Publication, Major
**Location:** Hamburg, Germany | **People:** Karl Marx, Friedrich Engels
Edited by Engels from Marx's manuscripts. Analyzes circulation of capital, introduces Department I/II distinction, conditions for simple and expanded reproduction.

### 33. Haymarket Affair (4 May 1886) — Historical, Major
**Location:** Chicago, USA | **People:** Albert Parsons, August Spies
Origin of International Workers' Day (May Day). Bomb thrown at police during eight-hour day rally; eight anarchist leaders convicted in political trial, four hanged. Second International designated May 1st as annual demonstration day in their honor.

### 34. First English Capital (1887) — Publication, Normal
**Location:** London, England | **People:** Karl Marx, Friedrich Engels
Moore/Aveling translation supervised by Engels. Brought Marx's critique to the heartland of industrial capitalism.

### 35. Second International Founded (14 Jul 1889) — Organization, Major
**Location:** Paris, France | **People:** Friedrich Engels
Founded on centenary of the French Revolution. United socialist and labor parties globally. Adopted May Day. Under it, mass parties grew enormously. But reformism, parliamentarism, and trade union bureaucracy undermined revolutionary commitment. Collapsed in August 1914.

### 36. Erfurt Program (1891) — Organization, Normal
**Location:** Erfurt, Germany | **People:** Karl Kautsky
Written by Kautsky. Structural flaw: separated "maximum programme" (revolution) from "minimum programme" (reforms), allowing the party to focus entirely on reforms while revolution receded into an indefinite future.

### 37. Second International Brussels Congress (3 Aug 1891) — Organization, Normal
**Location:** Brussels, Belgium | **People:** Friedrich Engels
Adopted resolution affirming necessity of political action by the working class. Further consolidated parliamentary socialist strategy.

### 38. Second International Zurich Congress (9 Aug 1893) — Organization, Normal
**Location:** Zurich, Switzerland | **People:** Friedrich Engels
Formally excluded anarchists, cementing commitment to political party organization. Engels attended — one of his last major public appearances.

### 39. Das Kapital Volume III Published (1894) — Publication, Major
**Location:** Hamburg, Germany | **People:** Karl Marx, Friedrich Engels
Final volume. Analyzes the tendential fall in the rate of profit — capitalism's own development undermines profitability, producing crises and ultimate historical limits. Published months before Engels's death.

### 40. Death of Friedrich Engels (5 Aug 1895) — Biography, Major
**Location:** London, England | **People:** Friedrich Engels
Died of throat cancer at 74. Independent contributions: *Anti-Dühring*, *Origin of the Family, Private Property and the State*. Last direct link to founding generation of Marxism.

### 41. RSDLP Founded (1898) — Organization, Major
**Location:** Minsk, Russian Empire | **People:** Vladimir Lenin
Nine delegates in a small wooden house; most arrested shortly after. Inauspicious beginning of the party that would make the October Revolution.

### 42. What Is To Be Done? (1902) — Publication, Critical
**Location:** Stuttgart, Germany | **People:** Vladimir Lenin
Foundational work on revolutionary party organization. Against "Economism": revolutionary consciousness must be brought to the class by a vanguard of professional revolutionaries. Established theoretical basis for the Bolshevik party conception.

### 43. Bolshevik-Menshevik Split (Jul 1903) — Organization, Critical
**Location:** London/Brussels | **People:** Vladimir Lenin, Julius Martov
RSDLP Second Congress. Immediate issue: definition of party membership. Deeper: nature of coming revolution, role of party, relationship to liberal bourgeoisie. Never healed — one of the most consequential organizational divisions in political history.

### 44. Second International Amsterdam Congress (14 Aug 1904) — Organization, Normal
**Location:** Amsterdam, Netherlands | **People:** Jean Jaurès, August Bebel
Major debate on "ministerialism" — whether socialists should join bourgeois governments. Adopted resolution against. Foreshadowed the collaboration vs. revolution question of 1914.

### 45. First Russian Revolution (Jan 1905) — Revolution, Critical
**Location:** St. Petersburg, Russia | **People:** Vladimir Lenin, Leon Trotsky
Bloody Sunday triggered strikes, peasant uprisings, military mutinies, and spontaneous formation of soviets. Trotsky chaired St. Petersburg Soviet. Lenin called it a "dress rehearsal" for 1917.

### 46. First Japanese Translation of Manifesto (1906) — Publication, Normal
**Location:** Japan | **People:** Sakai Toshihiko, Kotoku Shusui
Published in *Heimin Shimbun*. Marxism reaches Japan — part of its transformation into a truly global movement.

### 47. Second International Stuttgart Congress (18 Aug 1907) — Organization, Major
**Location:** Stuttgart, Germany | **People:** Rosa Luxemburg, Vladimir Lenin, August Bebel
Anti-militarism resolution: socialists must "utilize the crisis created by war to hasten the downfall of capitalist class rule." Luxemburg and Lenin amended it. The resolution became the measure of betrayal in 1914.

### 48. Second International Copenhagen Congress (28 Aug 1910) — Organization, Normal
**Location:** Copenhagen, Denmark | **People:** Clara Zetkin
Clara Zetkin proposed International Women's Day (8 March), adopted by congress. Women textile workers striking on that date in 1917 would trigger the February Revolution.

### 49. Bordiga Founds Circolo Carlo Marx (1912) — Organization, Normal
**Location:** Naples, Italy | **People:** Amadeo Bordiga
Revolutionary opposition to PSI reformism and Italy's colonial war in Libya. Nucleus of the Italian Communist Left.

### 50. Prague Conference (Jan 1912) — Organization, Major
**Location:** Prague, Austria-Hungary | **People:** Vladimir Lenin
Bolsheviks formally constitute as independent party. Organizational basis for October laid.

### 51. PSI Reggio Emilia Congress (1912) — Organization, Normal
**Location:** Reggio Emilia, Italy | **People:** Amadeo Bordiga, Benito Mussolini
Intransigent Revolutionary Fraction formed. Both Bordiga and young Mussolini (then still socialist) associated — later radically divergent paths.

### 52. Second International Basel Congress (24 Nov 1912) — Organization, Major
**Location:** Basel, Switzerland | **People:** Jean Jaurès, Rosa Luxemburg
Emergency congress on Balkan Wars and European war threat. Basel Manifesto: determination to prevent war and use conflict to accelerate capitalism's fall. Less than two years later, most parties that voted for it would support the war.

### 53. Marxism and the National Question (1913) — Publication, Normal
**Location:** Vienna, Austria-Hungary | **People:** Joseph Stalin, Vladimir Lenin
Stalin's systematic treatment of the national question, commissioned by Lenin. Against Austro-Marxist "national-cultural autonomy." Established Stalin's reputation on the national question.

### 54. PSI Milan Congress (1913) — Organization, Normal
**Location:** Milan, Italy | **People:** Amadeo Bordiga
Left tendency opposed parliamentary alliances with bourgeois parties. Bordiga continued developing anti-electoralist positions.

## V. THE GREAT RUPTURE (1914–1923)

### 55. World War I Begins (28 Jul 1914) — War, Critical
**Location:** Europe
Assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand triggered a chain of alliances plunging Europe into war. Over 17 million killed, four empires destroyed (Russian, German, Austro-Hungarian, Ottoman). For Marxists: inevitable product of inter-imperialist rivalry — competition between national capitals for markets and spheres of influence.

### 56. The Great Betrayal (4 Aug 1914) — Historical, Critical
**Location:** Berlin, Germany | **People:** Vladimir Lenin, Karl Kautsky
SPD votes unanimously for war credits. Second International collapses into social-chauvinism. Lenin initially refused to believe the news: "From now on, I am not a Social Democrat, I am a Communist." Demonstrated that decades of reformism had organically integrated socialist parties into the bourgeois state.

### 57. Zimmerwald Conference (Sep 1915) — Organization, Major
**Location:** Zimmerwald, Switzerland | **People:** Vladimir Lenin, Leon Trotsky
Anti-war socialists meet. Lenin organizes the Zimmerwald Left: "Turn the imperialist war into civil war." Nucleus of the future Third International. Lenin: "All the internationalists in the world could fit in two stagecoaches."

### 58. Easter Rising (Apr 1916) — Revolution, Major
**Location:** Dublin, Ireland | **People:** James Connolly
Irish Citizen Army — the only workers' militia — rises against British rule. Connolly, a Marxist, saw it as simultaneously national liberation and a step toward workers' power. Executed by firing squad strapped to a chair. Lenin defended the rising against critics who dismissed it as a "putsch."

### 59. Imperialism, the Highest Stage of Capitalism (1916) — Publication, Major
**Location:** Petrograd, Russia | **People:** Vladimir Lenin
Analyzes capitalism's development into monopoly capitalism and imperialism. WWI was not accidental but inevitable. Imperialism generates "super-profits" from colonial exploitation, allowing the bourgeoisie to bribe an "aristocracy of labour." Standard Marxist analysis of the world system.

### 60. Kienthal Conference (24 Apr 1916) — Organization, Major
**Location:** Kienthal, Switzerland | **People:** Vladimir Lenin, Robert Grimm
Second Zimmerwald conference. More militant manifesto: explicitly called on socialist deputies to vote against war credits and break with national unity governments. Reflected growing radicalization.

### 61. February Revolution (Feb 1917) — Revolution, Critical
**Location:** Petrograd, Russia | **People:** Nicholas II
Female textile workers strike on International Women's Day. Soldiers join. Tsar abdicates — 300+ years of Romanov rule ended. "Dual Power" emerges: Provisional Government vs. Petrograd Soviet. Unstable equilibrium resolved by October.

### 62. Lenin Returns — April Theses (Apr 1917) — Historical, Critical
**Location:** Petrograd, Russia | **People:** Vladimir Lenin
Returns via "sealed train." Issues April Theses at Finland Station: no support for Provisional Government, all power to the Soviets, end the war, nationalize land. Rejects orthodox position that Russia must complete bourgeois revolution first. Initially opposed by most Bolshevik leaders; prevailed within weeks.

### 63. July Days Uprising (16 Jul 1917) — Revolution, Major
**Location:** Petrograd, Russia | **People:** Vladimir Lenin
Spontaneous armed demonstrations demanding "All Power to the Soviets." Suppressed by Provisional Government. Lenin goes into hiding in Finland. Taught Bolsheviks that insurrection requires careful preparation and soviet majority — conditions met by October.

### 64. Kornilov Affair (Aug 1917) — Historical, Major
**Location:** Petrograd, Russia | **People:** Vladimir Lenin, Lavr Kornilov
General Kornilov attempts military coup. Bolsheviks organize defense, arm Red Guards. Coup collapses; Bolsheviks emerge as defenders of the revolution. Win majorities in Petrograd and Moscow soviets for the first time.

### 65. The October Revolution (25 Oct 1917 OS) — Revolution, Critical
**Location:** Petrograd, Russia | **People:** Vladimir Lenin, Leon Trotsky
First successful proletarian revolution. Bolsheviks seize Winter Palace. Second Congress of Soviets ratifies transfer of power. Lenin: "We shall now proceed to construct the socialist order." Transformed Marxism from theory into reality, inspiring movements worldwide.

### 66. Decree on Peace (8 Nov 1917) — Historical, Major
**Location:** Petrograd, Russia | **People:** Vladimir Lenin
First act of the Soviet government. Called on all belligerents for immediate peace "without annexations or indemnities." Addressed to peoples over the heads of their rulers. Bolsheviks making good on their promise to end Russia's participation in imperialist war.

### 67. Finnish Civil War (Jan 1918) — War, Major
**Location:** Finland | **People:** Carl Gustaf Mannerheim
Social Democratic Reds vs. conservative Whites with German military support. ~30,000 Reds died, many in camps. One of the first counter-revolutionary victories post-October — demonstrated bourgeoisie would fight with extreme brutality to prevent spread of soviet power.

### 68. Constituent Assembly Dissolved (5 Jan 1918) — Historical, Major
**Location:** Petrograd, Russia | **People:** Vladimir Lenin
Elected November 1917, met for one session. Bolsheviks (24% of vote) dissolved it, arguing soviet democracy was superior to bourgeois parliamentarism. One of the most debated acts of the revolution.

### 69. Treaty of Brest-Litovsk (Mar 1918) — Historical, Major
**Location:** Brest-Litovsk | **People:** Vladimir Lenin, Leon Trotsky
Harsh peace with Germany. Soviet Russia exits WWI. Left SRs revolt. Lenin argues the revolution needs a "breathing space." Surrendered vast territories (Finland, Baltic states, Poland, Ukraine) to preserve the revolution.

### 70. World War I Armistice (11 Nov 1918) — War, Critical
**Location:** Compiègne, France
Armistice signed in a railway carriage. 17+ million dead, old European order in ruins. Created conditions for revolutionary upheaval across the continent.

### 71. German Revolution (Nov 1918) — Revolution, Critical
**Location:** Germany | **People:** Rosa Luxemburg, Karl Liebknecht, Gustav Noske
Sailors mutiny in Kiel. Workers' and soldiers' councils form across Germany. Kaiser abdicates. SPD takes power to stop the revolution. Noske: "Someone must be the bloodhound."

### 72. Spartacist Uprising (Jan 1919) — Revolution, Critical
**Location:** Berlin, Germany | **People:** Rosa Luxemburg, Karl Liebknecht, Gustav Noske
KPD attempts insurrection. Rosa Luxemburg and Karl Liebknecht murdered by Freikorps on orders of SPD Minister Noske. "Order reigns in Berlin!" — Luxemburg's last article quoted the phrase to predict future revolution.

### 73. Third International Founded (Mar 1919) — Organization, Critical
**Location:** Moscow, Soviet Russia | **People:** Vladimir Lenin, Grigory Zinoviev
Lenin founds Communist International (Comintern). The call goes out for world revolution. Intended to replace the bankrupt Second International with a revolutionary organization that would coordinate communist parties worldwide.

### 74. Hungarian Soviet Republic (Mar 1919) — Revolution, Major
**Location:** Budapest, Hungary | **People:** Béla Kun
133 days of red power (March–August). Béla Kun leads short-lived communist state. Falls to Romanian invasion and internal counter-revolution. Demonstrated both the possibility and fragility of revolution outside Russia.

### 75. Bologna Congress — Abstentionist Fraction Formed (1919) — Organization, Major
**Location:** Bologna, Italy | **People:** Amadeo Bordiga
At PSI congress, Bordiga formed the Abstentionist Communist Fraction — boycotting bourgeois parliamentary elections, demanding PSI align with the Comintern. Organizational precursor to the founding of PCd'I.

### 76. May Fourth Movement (4 May 1919) — Revolution, Major
**Location:** Beijing, China | **People:** Chen Duxiu, Li Dazhao
Students demonstrated against Treaty of Versailles transferring German concessions in Shandong to Japan. Spread nationwide, catalyzing broad intellectual and political awakening. Created conditions for the spread of Marxism in China.

### 77. Biennio Rosso Begins (Sep 1919) — Revolution, Major
**Location:** Turin, Italy | **People:** Antonio Gramsci
Red Years in Italy. Turin workers occupy Fiat factories, run them through Factory Councils. Gramsci's *L'Ordine Nuovo* leads the movement. The high point of Italian revolutionary activity.

### 78. Manifesto Translated to Chinese (1920) — Publication, Major
**Location:** China | **People:** Chen Wangdao, Chen Duxiu
Chen Wangdao translates the Manifesto — supposedly eating ink by mistake in his cold shed. Seed for the Chinese Communist Party planted.

### 79. Communist Groups Established Across China (1920) — Organization, Normal
**Location:** China | **People:** Chen Duxiu, Li Dazhao, Mao Zedong
Marxist study circles form in Shanghai, Beijing, Wuhan, Changsha, Guangzhou, and among Chinese students abroad. Organizational basis for the CPC founding.

### 80. Second Congress of Comintern (Jul 1920) — Organization, Major
**Location:** Moscow, Soviet Russia | **People:** Vladimir Lenin
Lenin presents 21 Conditions for membership, enforcing split with reformists. Comintern defines itself against social democracy. Only parties accepting centralized discipline and revolutionary programme admitted.

### 81. Imola Conference of the Italian Left (28 Nov 1920) — Organization, Major
**Location:** Imola, Italy | **People:** Amadeo Bordiga
Bordiga's Abstentionist Fraction merged with other left currents to form the Unified Communist Fraction, preparing the ground for the founding of the PCd'I at Livorno two months later.

### 82. Communist Party of Italy Founded (Jan 1921) — Organization, Major
**Location:** Livorno, Italy | **People:** Amadeo Bordiga, Antonio Gramsci
Bordiga's faction walks out of PSI congress singing the Internationale. Italian Comintern section born. Bordiga elected first general secretary.

### 83. First National Congress of Chinese Communist Party (23 Jul 1921) — Organization, Critical
**Location:** Shanghai/Jiaxing, China | **People:** Mao Zedong, Chen Duxiu, Li Dazhao
CPC founded. 13 delegates representing ~57 members, meeting secretly in Shanghai's French Concession, later moving to a boat to avoid police. Beginning of one of history's most consequential political organizations.

### 84. Third Congress of Comintern (22 Jun 1921) — Organization, Major
**Location:** Moscow, Soviet Russia | **People:** Vladimir Lenin, Leon Trotsky
Repudiated "theory of the offensive" after disastrous German March Action. Adopted watchword "To the masses!" — communist parties must win workers' confidence before attempting revolution.

### 85. Secretariat of Chinese Labour Organization (Aug 1921) — Organization, Normal
**Location:** Shanghai, China | **People:** Zhang Guotao
First nationwide labor organizing body under CPC leadership. Beginning of systematic communist-led labor organizing in China.

### 86. Kronstadt Rebellion (Mar 1921) — Revolution, Major
**Location:** Kronstadt, Soviet Russia | **People:** Vladimir Lenin, Leon Trotsky
Sailors demand "Soviets without Bolsheviks." Suppressed by Red Army. Exposed contradictions of War Communism and the growing gap between the Bolshevik state and the working class it claimed to represent.

### 87. NEP Introduced (Mar 1921) — Economic, Major
**Location:** Soviet Russia | **People:** Vladimir Lenin
New Economic Policy allows limited market relations: "One step backward, two steps forward." Retreat from War Communism necessitated by economic collapse. Introduced limited private trade and small-scale enterprise while maintaining state ownership of commanding heights.

### 88. Hong Kong Seamen's Strike (12 Jan 1922) — Revolution, Normal
**Location:** Hong Kong, China
Major strike by Chinese maritime workers demanding wage increases. Lasted until March. Demonstrated growing working-class militancy and influence of the nascent communist movement on Chinese labor.

### 89. First National Labour Conference (1 May 1922) — Organization, Normal
**Location:** Guangzhou, China
First attempt to coordinate the Chinese labor movement nationally under communist influence.

### 90. First Congress of Chinese Socialist Youth League (5 May 1922) — Organization, Normal
**Location:** Guangzhou, China
Established organizational framework for recruiting young people into the communist movement in China.

### 91. Second National Congress of CPC (16 Jul 1922) — Organization, Major
**Location:** Shanghai, China | **People:** Chen Duxiu
Adopted minimum programme (anti-imperialism, anti-feudalism, democratic republic) and maximum programme (socialism/communism). Formally voted to join the Comintern.

### 92. Fourth Congress of Comintern (5 Nov 1922) — Organization, Major
**Location:** Moscow, Soviet Russia | **People:** Vladimir Lenin, Amadeo Bordiga, Leon Trotsky
Formally adopted United Front tactic. Bordiga presented report on fascism in Italy, analyzing its class character. Last congress Lenin was able to attend.

### 93. Third National Congress of CPC (12 Jun 1923) — Organization, Major
**Location:** Guangzhou, China | **People:** Chen Duxiu, Mao Zedong
Adopted decision for CPC members to join the Kuomintang as individuals — "bloc within" united front strategy directed by the Comintern against imperialism and warlordism.

### 94. Failed German October (Oct 1923) — Revolution, Major
**Location:** Hamburg, Germany | **People:** Ernst Thälmann
KPD planned insurrection, called off last minute. Only Hamburg rises under Thälmann, is suppressed. Revolutionary wave recedes in Europe. Beginning of the end of the post-1917 revolutionary period.

## VI. COUNTER-REVOLUTION (1924–1940)

### 95. Death of Lenin (21 Jan 1924) — Biography, Critical
**Location:** Gorki, Soviet Russia | **People:** Vladimir Lenin, Joseph Stalin, Leon Trotsky
Lenin dies at 53 after strokes. His death triggered the power struggle that would define the Soviet Union's trajectory. Stalin promoted "Socialism in One Country," breaking with internationalism. Body placed in mausoleum against the wishes of Lenin's widow. Lenin's "Testament" warning against Stalin was suppressed by the party leadership.

### 96. Como Conference of PCd'I (May 1924) — Organization, Normal
**Location:** Como, Italy | **People:** Amadeo Bordiga
Italian Left faction won majority on party direction despite Comintern pressure, demonstrating continued support for Bordiga's positions within the party.

### 97. Fifth Congress of Comintern (17 Jun 1924) — Organization, Major
**Location:** Moscow, Soviet Union | **People:** Grigory Zinoviev, Amadeo Bordiga
Adopted "Bolshevisation" — requiring all parties to reorganize on factory cells rather than territorial sections. Criticized by Italian Left as bureaucratic imposition severing parties from their organic traditions. Marked growing Stalinization of the Comintern.

### 98. Trotsky Removed as Military Commissar (Jan 1925) — Historical, Major
**Location:** Moscow, Soviet Union | **People:** Leon Trotsky, Joseph Stalin
Trotsky lost his primary institutional base of power. Decisive moment in the internal party struggle following Lenin's death. Beginning of the systematic dismantling of the Left Opposition.

### 99. ECCI Orders Elimination of Bordiga Tendency (Mar 1925) — Organization, Major
**Location:** Moscow, Soviet Union | **People:** Amadeo Bordiga, Grigory Zinoviev
Comintern Executive ordered suppression of the Italian Left faction that had founded the Communist Party of Italy. Part of the broader pattern of Stalinization across the international communist movement.

### 100. Committee of Intesa Formed (Apr 1925) — Organization, Normal
**Location:** Italy | **People:** Amadeo Bordiga
Italian Left organized internal opposition to Comintern's bolshevisation directives and the transfer of party leadership away from Bordiga's faction.

### 101. Lyon Congress of PCd'I (20 Jan 1926) — Organization, Major
**Location:** Lyon, France | **People:** Amadeo Bordiga, Antonio Gramsci, Palmiro Togliatti
Party leadership transferred from Bordiga's faction to the centre group led by Gramsci and Togliatti, in line with Comintern directives. Italian Left presented its theses but was defeated. Decisive turning point for Italian communism.

### 102. Bordiga's Lyons Theses (1926) — Publication, Major
**Location:** Italy | **People:** Amadeo Bordiga
Most comprehensive formal articulation of the Italian Left Communist position on party organization, the revolutionary process, and the relationship between national parties and the International. Remained the programmatic reference for the Italian Communist Left.

### 103. Bordiga Confronts Stalin (Feb 1926) — Historical, Major
**Location:** Moscow, Soviet Union | **People:** Amadeo Bordiga, Joseph Stalin
At ECCI Sixth Plenum, Bordiga declared the Russian Party must be subject to the International, not vice versa. The last significant open challenge to Stalin's authority from within the Comintern's own structures. Bordiga was subsequently marginalized.

### 104. Seventh Enlarged Plenum of ECCI (Nov 1926) — Organization, Critical
**Location:** Moscow, Soviet Union | **People:** Joseph Stalin, Amadeo Bordiga
Stalin formally presented "Socialism in One Country" — decisive break with the internationalist orientation of the early Comintern. PCd'I delegation expelled from Comintern leadership structures. Marked the definitive Stalinization of the International.

### 105. Shanghai Massacre (12 Apr 1927) — Historical, Critical
**Location:** Shanghai, China | **People:** Chiang Kai-shek, Joseph Stalin
Chiang Kai-shek's forces slaughtered thousands of communists and trade unionists. Stalin's policy of subordinating the CPC to the Kuomintang was directly responsible — the CPC had been ordered to maintain the alliance even as the right wing of the KMT prepared to strike. One of the most devastating consequences of Stalinist foreign policy.

### 106. Wuhan KMT Expels Communists (15 Jul 1927) — Historical, Critical
**Location:** Wuhan, China | **People:** Wang Jingwei
First United Front collapsed completely. The "left" KMT faction also expelled all communists. CPC driven underground and into the countryside. Complete vindication of those who had warned against the "bloc within" strategy.

### 107. Nanchang Uprising (1 Aug 1927) — Revolution, Critical
**Location:** Nanchang, China | **People:** Zhou Enlai, He Long, Zhu De
Beginning of CPC armed struggle. Forces under Zhou Enlai launched armed uprising. Rebels forced to retreat within days, but the event is celebrated as the founding of the Chinese Red Army. August 1 remains PRC Army Day.

### 108. Emergency Conference of CPC (7 Aug 1927) — Organization, Major
**Location:** Hankou, China | **People:** Mao Zedong, Qu Qiubai
Adopted new line of armed insurrection and agrarian revolution. Mao argued for mobilizing the peasantry as the main revolutionary force — a decisive strategic shift from urban to rural revolution.

### 109. Autumn Harvest Uprisings (Sep 1927) — Revolution, Major
**Location:** Hunan, China | **People:** Mao Zedong
Mao led peasant insurrections in Hunan. After initial military failures, led surviving forces to the Jinggang Mountains — establishing the first rural revolutionary base area and pioneering peasant guerrilla warfare strategy.

### 110. Italian Left Fraction Formed in Exile (1927) — Organization, Major
**Location:** Pantin, France | **People:** Amadeo Bordiga
Members of the Italian Communist Left in emigration formed the Left Fraction, continuing to uphold Italian Left positions against the Stalinized Comintern. Published theoretical journals and maintained organizational continuity through the exile years.

### 111. Trotsky Exiled to Alma-Ata (1928) — Biography, Major
**Location:** Alma-Ata, Kazakhstan | **People:** Leon Trotsky, Joseph Stalin
Left Opposition crushed in the USSR. Trotsky exiled to Kazakhstan, then expelled from the Soviet Union entirely in 1929. Spent his remaining years organizing the Fourth International and writing critical analyses of Stalinism.

### 112. Sixth Congress of Comintern (18 Jun 1928) — Organization, Major
**Location:** Moscow, Soviet Union | **People:** Nikolai Bukharin, Joseph Stalin
Adopted "Third Period" theory: imminent revolutionary crisis predicted; social-democratic parties characterized as "social-fascist" — the main enemy. This ultra-left line would prove catastrophic in Germany, preventing a united front against Nazism.

### 113. Sixth National Congress of CPC (Jul 1928) — Organization, Major
**Location:** Moscow, Soviet Union | **People:** Qu Qiubai, Zhou Enlai
Only CPC congress held outside China. Held in Moscow under Comintern supervision. Endorsed peasant-based strategy while formally maintaining leading role of the proletariat.

### 114. Bukharin Removed (1929) — Historical, Major
**Location:** Moscow, Soviet Union | **People:** Nikolai Bukharin, Joseph Stalin
Right Opposition crushed. Bukharin removed from Politburo. Stalin begins forced collectivization — resulting in millions of deaths from famine, particularly in Ukraine (Holodomor). All opposition to Stalin within the party now eliminated.

### 115. Wall Street Crash (29 Oct 1929) — Economic, Critical
**Location:** New York, USA
Triggered the Great Depression — worst economic crisis in capitalist history. Mass unemployment, bank failures, industrial collapse worldwide. Vindicated Marxist analysis of capitalism's contradictions. Created conditions for the rise of fascism in Europe.

### 116. Eleventh Plenum of ECCI (Mar 1931) — Organization, Normal
**Location:** Moscow, Soviet Union
Reinforced "class against class" line, deepening sectarian orientation that isolated communist parties from potential allies against fascism.

### 117. 1844 Manuscripts Published (1932) — Publication, Major
**Location:** Moscow/Berlin | **People:** Karl Marx
Discovery and publication of Marx's *Economic and Philosophic Manuscripts of 1844*. "Young Marx" — humanism, alienation, species-being — entered discourse. Influenced the Frankfurt School and generated debate about continuity vs. break between "young" and "mature" Marx.

### 118. Hitler Takes Power (Jan 1933) — Historical, Critical
**Location:** Berlin, Germany | **People:** Adolf Hitler, Ernst Thälmann
KPD, following Stalin's "Third Period" theory, refused united front with SPD against Nazism ("After Hitler, us!"). The strongest Communist Party in Europe outside the USSR was destroyed without resistance. Thälmann arrested, later executed in Buchenwald. The most catastrophic consequence of Stalinist sectarianism.

### 119. Thirteenth Plenum of ECCI (Dec 1933) — Organization, Normal
**Location:** Moscow, Soviet Union
Analyzed fascism as "the open terrorist dictatorship of the most reactionary, most chauvinistic and most imperialist elements of finance capital." Formulation later refined at the Seventh Congress.

### 120. Long March Begins (Oct 1934) — War, Critical
**Location:** Jiangxi, China | **People:** Mao Zedong, Zhou Enlai, Zhu De
~86,000 Red Army soldiers broke through Nationalist encirclement in the Fifth Encirclement Campaign. The march would cover ~9,000 kilometers over one year. Defining experience of the CPC leadership generation.

### 121. Zunyi Conference (Jan 1935) — Organization, Critical
**Location:** Zunyi, China | **People:** Mao Zedong, Zhou Enlai, Wang Jiaxiang
Turning point in CPC history during the Long March. Mao gained effective control of military decision-making, displacing Comintern-backed leadership that had led to disastrous defeats. Beginning of Mao's ascendancy.

### 122. Seventh Congress of Comintern (25 Jul 1935) — Organization, Major
**Location:** Moscow, Soviet Union | **People:** Georgi Dimitrov, Joseph Stalin
Final regular Comintern congress. Adopted Popular Front policy — alliances with bourgeois democratic parties against fascism. Dramatic reversal of Third Period line. Dimitrov presented main report.

### 123. Long March Concludes (Oct 1935) — Historical, Major
**Location:** Yan'an, China | **People:** Mao Zedong
Surviving forces arrived in Yan'an, Shaanxi. Of ~86,000 who began, only ~8,000 completed the march. Yan'an served as CPC base for the next decade.

### 124. CPC Declaration on Resisting Japan (Feb 1936) — Publication, Normal
**Location:** Yan'an, China | **People:** Mao Zedong
Called for broad national united front against Japanese aggression. Strategic shift from class war to national defense.

### 125. Spanish Civil War Begins (Jul 1936) — War, Critical
**Location:** Spain | **People:** Francisco Franco
Workers defeat Franco's coup in Barcelona and Madrid. Anarchist (CNT-FAI) and POUM militias form. "¡No pasarán!" The Spanish Civil War became the great cause of the international left — the last stand against fascism before world war.

### 126. Xi'an Incident (12 Dec 1936) — Historical, Critical
**Location:** Xi'an, China | **People:** Chiang Kai-shek, Zhang Xueliang, Zhou Enlai
Chiang Kai-shek detained by his own generals demanding an end to civil war and a united front against Japan. Zhou Enlai negotiated for the CPC. Chiang released after agreeing to cooperate. Led to the Second United Front.

### 127. May Days in Barcelona (May 1937) — Revolution, Major
**Location:** Barcelona, Spain | **People:** Andreu Nin, George Orwell
PSUC (Stalinist) and Republican police crush the revolution within the republic. Andreu Nin (POUM leader) tortured and killed by Soviet agents. Orwell narrowly escapes. Demonstrated that Stalinism was prepared to destroy the revolution to maintain control.

### 128. The Great Terror (1937) — Historical, Critical
**Location:** Moscow, Soviet Union | **People:** Joseph Stalin, Grigory Zinoviev, Nikolai Bukharin
Stalin liquidated Old Bolsheviks. Zinoviev, Kamenev, Bukharin executed after show trials. Red Army decimated (Tukhachevsky). The generation that had made the October Revolution was physically annihilated by the counter-revolution from within.

### 129. Marco Polo Bridge Incident (7 Jul 1937) — War, Critical
**Location:** Beijing, China
Skirmish between Chinese and Japanese troops escalated into full-scale war. Beginning of the Second Sino-Japanese War, merging into World War II.

### 130. Second United Front Formally Announced (22 Sep 1937) — Organization, Major
**Location:** China | **People:** Chiang Kai-shek, Mao Zedong, Zhou Enlai
KMT-CPC cooperation against Japan. Red Army reorganized as Eighth Route Army under nominal Nationalist command. Uneasy alliance that would collapse after the defeat of Japan.

### 131. Nanjing Massacre (13 Dec 1937) — War, Critical
**Location:** Nanjing, China
Japanese forces captured Nanjing. In the following weeks, mass killings, rape, and destruction. Estimates: tens of thousands to 300,000+ killed. One of the worst atrocities of the 20th century.

### 132. Sixth Plenary Session of CPC Central Committee (29 Sep 1938) — Organization, Major
**Location:** Yan'an, China | **People:** Mao Zedong
Consolidated Mao's leadership. Mao called for "sinification of Marxism" — adapting communist theory to Chinese conditions.

### 133. Grundrisse Published (1939) — Publication, Major
**Location:** Moscow, Soviet Union | **People:** Karl Marx
Limited Moscow edition of Marx's 1857–58 notebooks. Only became widely known in the West in the 1970s. Revolutionized understanding of Marx's value theory — contains his most extensive treatment of abstract labor, money, and the "fragment on machines."

### 134. Franco Wins Spanish Civil War (1 Apr 1939) — War, Major
**Location:** Spain | **People:** Francisco Franco
Republic falls. Hundreds of thousands flee into exile. Revolution definitively crushed. Last hope of stopping fascism through popular resistance extinguished.

### 135. World War II Begins in Europe (1 Sep 1939) — War, Critical
**Location:** Poland | **People:** Adolf Hitler
Germany invaded Poland. Britain and France declare war. The conflict would kill 70–85 million people and fundamentally reshape the world order.

### 136. Assassination of Trotsky (20 Aug 1940) — Biography, Critical
**Location:** Coyoacán, Mexico | **People:** Leon Trotsky, Ramon Mercader
Ramon Mercader (NKVD agent) killed Trotsky with an ice axe. Last major Old Bolshevik eliminated. Trotsky's final words to followers: "I am sure of the victory of the Fourth International. Go forward!"

## VII. WORLD WAR II & COMINTERN DISSOLUTION (1941–1945)

### 137. Germany Invades Soviet Union (22 Jun 1941) — War, Critical
**Location:** Soviet Union | **People:** Adolf Hitler, Joseph Stalin
Operation Barbarossa — the largest military invasion in history. 3.8 million troops along a 2,900-kilometer front. The Eastern Front became the decisive theater of WWII, where the Wehrmacht was ultimately ground down and destroyed. The Soviet Union suffered 27 million dead — the greatest sacrifice of any nation in the war.

### 138. Japan Attacks Pearl Harbor (7 Dec 1941) — War, Critical
**Location:** Pearl Harbor, Hawaii
Surprise attack on the US naval base brought the United States into WWII, transforming it into a truly global conflict. The Pacific War would end with the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki.

### 139. Yan'an Rectification Movement (1 Feb 1942) — Organization, Major
**Location:** Yan'an, China | **People:** Mao Zedong
Mass ideological campaign to consolidate Mao's leadership and establish Mao Zedong Thought as the party's guiding ideology. Combined study, criticism, and self-criticism. Set the pattern for future CPC political campaigns.

### 140. Communist International Formally Dissolved (22 May 1943) — Organization, Critical
**Location:** Moscow, Soviet Union | **People:** Joseph Stalin
Comintern dissolved by ECCI resolution. Stalin proposed dissolution ostensibly to demonstrate goodwill to Western Allies. Definitive end of the organizational framework of world revolution established by Lenin in 1919. National communist parties were now fully subordinated to Soviet foreign policy without even the pretense of an international structure.

### 141. Internationalist Communist Party of Italy Founded (1943) — Organization, Major
**Location:** Italy | **People:** Onorato Damen, Bruno Maffi
PCInt (Partito Comunista Internazionalista) established by Damen, Maffi, and returning exiles. Sought to continue the Italian Communist Left tradition outside the Stalinist framework. Re-established left communist organizational presence in Italy.

### 142. Seventh National Congress of CPC (23 Apr 1945) — Organization, Major
**Location:** Yan'an, China | **People:** Mao Zedong, Liu Shaoqi, Zhou Enlai
Formally adopted Mao Zedong Thought as guiding ideology alongside Marxism-Leninism. Prepared the party for the final confrontation with the Kuomintang. CPC membership had grown from ~40,000 in 1937 to over 1.2 million.

### 143. Japan Surrenders (15 Aug 1945) — War, Critical
**Location:** Tokyo, Japan | **People:** Hirohito
Unconditional surrender following atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki and Soviet declaration of war. End of WWII in Asia. Created conditions for the final phase of the Chinese Civil War.

## VIII. CHINESE CIVIL WAR & VICTORY (1945–1949)

### 144. Mao Arrives in Chongqing for Negotiations (28 Aug 1945) — Historical, Major
**Location:** Chongqing, China | **People:** Mao Zedong, Chiang Kai-shek
First Mao-Chiang meeting since the war. Despite signing the "Double Tenth Agreement" (Oct 10), negotiations failed to prevent resumption of full-scale civil war.

### 145. Full-Scale Chinese Civil War Resumes (Jun 1946) — War, Major
**Location:** China | **People:** Mao Zedong, Chiang Kai-shek
Resumed after breakdown of American-mediated ceasefire. Nationalists initially held significant advantage in manpower and equipment, but CPC's land reform programme won massive peasant support.

### 146. CPC Central Committee Evacuates Yan'an (Mar 1947) — War, Major
**Location:** Yan'an/Shaanxi, China | **People:** Mao Zedong, Zhou Enlai
Facing major Nationalist offensive, CPC evacuated Yan'an (base for nearly a decade). Mao established mobile headquarters in Shaanxi hills, demonstrating the party's resilience and flexibility.

### 147. Three Great Campaigns (Sep 1948) — War, Critical
**Location:** Manchuria/North China | **People:** Mao Zedong, Lin Biao, Chen Yi, Deng Xiaoping
Liaoshen, Huaihai, and Pingjin campaigns (Sep 1948 – Jan 1949) were the decisive battles. CPC destroyed the main Nationalist armies, gaining control of Manchuria, the Yangtze-Huai region, and North China including Beijing and Tianjin.

### 148. Beijing Captured by CPC Forces (31 Jan 1949) — War, Major
**Location:** Beijing, China | **People:** Lin Biao, Fu Zuoyi
Former imperial capital taken after peaceful surrender of Nationalist garrison commander. Powerful symbolic victory.

### 149. CPC Forces Cross the Yangtze (21 Apr 1949) — War, Critical
**Location:** Nanjing, China | **People:** Mao Zedong
CPC crossed the Yangtze along a broad front. Nanjing, the Nationalist capital, captured April 23. Effective end of Nationalist resistance on the mainland.

### 150. Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference (21 Sep 1949) — Organization, Major
**Location:** Beijing, China | **People:** Mao Zedong, Zhou Enlai
Adopted the Common Programme as provisional constitution. Established the political framework for the People's Republic.

### 151. Chinese Revolution — PRC Proclaimed (1 Oct 1949) — Revolution, Critical
**Location:** Beijing, China | **People:** Mao Zedong
"The Chinese people have stood up!" Mao proclaimed the People's Republic from Tiananmen Gate. The world's largest revolution by population — the peasantry surrounded the cities. Fundamentally altered the global balance of power.

## IX. POST-WAR & NATIONAL LIBERATION (1952–1991)

### 152. Bordiga's Dialogue with Stalin (1952) — Publication, Major
**Location:** Italy | **People:** Amadeo Bordiga
Bordiga wrote *Economic and Social Structure of Russia Today*, proving the USSR was capitalist: wage labor, money, commodity exchange all remained. One of the earliest systematic analyses of the Soviet Union as state-capitalist.

### 153. Hungarian Revolution (Oct 1956) — Revolution, Major
**Location:** Budapest, Hungary | **People:** Imre Nagy
Workers' councils demanded "Socialism without Russians." Crushed by Soviet tanks. 2,500 Hungarians died defending the revolution. Demonstrated that opposition to bureaucratic rule could take the form of workers' self-organization — soviets against the "Soviet" state.

### 154. Cuban Revolution (1 Jan 1959) — Revolution, Critical
**Location:** Havana, Cuba | **People:** Fidel Castro, Che Guevara
26th of July Movement triumphed. Batista fled. Socialism established 90 miles from the USA. The Cuban Revolution inspired national liberation movements across Latin America and Africa and became a focal point of Cold War confrontation.

### 155. Assassination of Patrice Lumumba (17 Jan 1961) — Historical, Major
**Location:** Katanga, Congo | **People:** Patrice Lumumba
CIA and Belgium eliminated Congo's first democratically elected leader. Lumumba became a martyr of anti-imperialism, symbolizing the betrayal of African independence by Western powers and their local compradors.

### 156. Cultural Revolution Begins (1966) — Historical, Major
**Location:** China | **People:** Mao Zedong
"Bombard the Headquarters!" Mao mobilized Red Guards in a chaotic attempt to purge rivals and manage contradictions of state capitalism. "To rebel is justified!" The Cultural Revolution (1966–1976) devastated Chinese society, education, and culture, while claiming to defend revolutionary purity.

### 157. May '68 in France (May 1968) — Revolution, Major
**Location:** Paris, France | **People:** Daniel Cohn-Bendit
Students occupied the Sorbonne; 10 million workers joined a general strike — the largest in French history. "Be realistic, demand the impossible!" The PCF (French Communist Party) helped de-escalate the strike to save the Gaullist state, demonstrating the counter-revolutionary role of Stalinist parties in the advanced capitalist countries.

### 158. Fall of Saigon (30 Apr 1975) — Historical, Major
**Location:** Saigon, Vietnam | **People:** Ho Chi Minh
Vietnam unified after decades of war. The most powerful military machine in history defeated by a peasant army. The fall of Saigon marked the end of the American war in Vietnam and the limits of US imperial power.

### 159. Cambodian Genocide (1976) — Historical, Major
**Location:** Cambodia | **People:** Pol Pot
Khmer Rouge's "Year Zero." Pol Pot attempted a primitive agrarian utopia by emptying cities. 1.5–2 million died from execution, starvation, and forced labor. A distorted ideology mixing Maoism with French existentialism, representing one of the 20th century's worst atrocities.

### 160. Assassination of Thomas Sankara (15 Oct 1987) — Biography, Major
**Location:** Ouagadougou, Burkina Faso | **People:** Thomas Sankara, Blaise Compaoré
"Africa's Che Guevara" killed by deputy Blaise Compaoré, backed by France. The "Upright Man" had vaccinated 2.5 million children in a week, planted 10 million trees, banned female genital mutilation, and challenged French neo-colonialism. His elimination ensured the continuation of Françafrique dependency.

### 161. Dissolution of the USSR (26 Dec 1991) — Historical, Critical
**Location:** Moscow, Russia | **People:** Mikhail Gorbachev, Boris Yeltsin
Red flag lowered over the Kremlin. The Russian state-capitalist experiment, born from the degeneration of the October Revolution, ended — not through workers' revolution but through the conversion of the bureaucratic ruling class into a private bourgeoisie. Transition to "bandit capitalism" under Yeltsin devastated the living standards of the Russian working class. End of an era — but not the end of the class struggle.
